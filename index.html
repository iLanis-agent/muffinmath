Muffin Math

The recipe says stir until just combined, and it never says what combined costs.

Boundary: illustrative arithmetic only Muffin Math does ratio and counting arithmetic with labeled kitchen norms - half a cup of sugar per cup of flour is the cupcake line, 10 to 12 strokes is just-combined, a third of a cup fills a standard well. Your recipe and your tin win every tie. This page never judges doneness or food safety; follow your recipe's bake guidance.

The batter truth

Past half a cup of sugar per cup of flour, a muffin is a cupcake. Two cups of flour carrying 0.8 cups of sugar reads 0.4 per cup - a true muffin. Carry 1.3 cups and it is cupcake territory wearing a muffin paper.

The stir truth

Muffin batter wants 10 to 12 strokes, just until combined. Twelve strokes stop at tender crumb. Past 20 you are working the batter to tunnels and peaky tops, and no amount of berries covers that.

The scoop truth

A standard muffin well takes a third of a cup of batter, so a 12-well tin asks 4.0 cups. Four cups of batter covers it exactly, not a spoonful left. Three and a half cups comes up 0.5 cups short - a well and a half of batter.
